What it does for you

Protected, informed, and free to choose.

NOBAD VPN sends your traffic through a locked tunnel to a server you choose, so the network you're on can't read it. If something breaks, it tells you why.

01 / Protection

Nothing slips past the tunnel.

Your connection stays covered, even when the network around it doesn't behave.

  • Kill switch. Blocks every connection that doesn't go through the tunnel, and stays armed through drops, sleep and Wi-Fi changes.
  • DNS leak block. On by default: while you're connected, DNS can only leave through the tunnel.
02 / Network intelligence

Knows why a site fails.

"The site doesn't open" turns into evidence: which layer failed and what will actually fix it.

  • Layer by layer. Compares your network's DNS with independent resolvers, then tests TCP, TLS and HTTP to find the exact layer that broke.
  • Evil Twin check. Every network you join is graded A to F, and the VPN turns on by itself below the grade you choose.
03 / Freedom

Your servers, your route.

NOBAD is the app, not a server company. You decide where your traffic goes.

  • Bring any WireGuard server. Import the .conf from your provider or your own server.
  • Free servers in 2 minutes. Get your own free Proton VPN servers; NOBAD adds them from your Downloads folder by itself.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Still stuck? Support has the rest.

Does NOBAD VPN come with servers?

No. NOBAD VPN is a client: it connects to the WireGuard servers in the configs you import, from your provider or your own server. If you don't have any, the free servers guide gets you your own in about 2 minutes.

Is it really free?

Yes. No price, no sign-up, no ads. The code is open source under the MIT License.

Which computers does it run on?

Windows 10 version 1809 or later and Windows 11, 64-bit. ARM64 PCs run the app through Windows' x64 emulation and use the native ARM64 WireGuard. macOS, Linux and phones aren't supported yet.

Does it keep logs?

The app keeps its logs, usage and diagnostic history on your PC, and crash reports are off unless you opt in. The server-side logging policy is your VPN provider's.

What does it connect to?

Your VPN servers. At launch, a connectivity check (a ping to 1.1.1.1 and one test DNS lookup) and, unless you switch it off, IP-location lookups via ipinfo.io for your exit address and the server map. Everything else runs only when you start it or opt in.

Why does it need administrator rights?

The kill switch and DNS leak block use Windows Firewall rules. After setup, a launcher task means no UAC prompt. Adapter settings are only changed by the smart fix agent, after you switch it on, and every change is recorded so it can be undone.

Can I use it anywhere?

VPN use, and streaming services' own terms, vary by country and by service. Using a VPN doesn't change which laws or terms apply to you, so check them for where you are. NOBAD VPN is provided "as is" under the MIT License, without warranty.
